## Research Vision

My long-term research goal is to understand how neural organization and neuroplasticity contribute to variability in language and reading development. Across my research experiences, I have been interested in understanding how differences in neural organization, cognitive processes, and experiential factors contribute to individual variability in learning and behavior.

Building on my previous work in ASD, DLD, mathematical cognition, and memory, I am particularly interested in how short-term experience-dependent neural changes may accumulate into long-term developmental outcomes. Ultimately, I aim to investigate whether educational interventions and learning experiences can reshape neural systems underlying language and reading, and how these changes translate into meaningful improvements in learning outcomes across both typical and atypical populations.

## Selected Previous Research Experiences



### Project 3. Language and Face Lateralization in Autism Spectrum Disorder

`fTCD` `Neural Organization` `Atypical Development`



**Core Question:** How does neural organization for language and face processing differ in children with autism spectrum disorder (ASD)?



**Key Finding:** Typically developing children around age 10 showed a trend toward left-lateralized language processing, whereas age-matched children with ASD showed a right-lateralized tendency. In contrast, face lateralization appeared relatively stable across groups, suggesting that neural specialization for face processing may emerge earlier than language specialization.



**Methods**

- Functional Transcranial Doppler Sonography (fTCD)

- Language and face recognition paradigms

- Laterality Index analysis



**Output**

- APSSLH Conference Poster Presentation (Vietnam)

### Project 5. Language Anxiety Among Exchange Students

`Language Learning` `Individual Differences` `Cross-Cultural Research`



**Core Question:** How is language anxiety related to language achievement among Mandarin-speaking exchange students?



**Key Finding:** Students reporting the highest levels of language anxiety did not necessarily demonstrate the lowest language achievement. Relationships between anxiety and performance varied across listening, speaking, reading, and writing, highlighting substantial individual variability in second-language learning outcomes.



**Methods**

- Survey research

- Quantitative analysis

- Cross-cultural research design



**Output**

- ICEI Conference Poster Presentation (Taiwan)

### Project 6. Numerical Cognition and Distance Effect

`Mathematical Cognition` `Behavioral Research` `Experimental Design`



**Core Question:** How do numerical representations and problem-solving strategies influence arithmetic processing?



**Contribution:** Independently designed a PsychoPy-based experimental paradigm and collected behavioral data from 50 university students.



**Impact:** This project sparked my interest in rigorous experimental design and revealed how individuals often adopt distinct cognitive strategies when solving the same numerical task, leading to substantial variation in response time and accuracy.



**Methods**
