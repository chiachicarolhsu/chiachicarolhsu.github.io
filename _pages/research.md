<article class="research-project" aria-labelledby="project-3-language-and-face-lateralization-in-autism-spectrum-disorder">
<p class="research-project__meta">Previous · Functional Transcranial Doppler Ultrasound</p>
<h3 id="project-3-language-and-face-lateralization-in-autism-spectrum-disorder">Language and Face Lateralization in Autism Spectrum Disorder</h3>
<ul class="research-project__keywords" aria-label="Research keywords"><li>Autism Spectrum Disorder</li><li>Language Lateralization</li><li>Face Recognition</li><li>Reading Ability</li></ul>
<p class="research-project__summary">How do language and face lateralization differ across autistic children, typically developing children, and adults?</p>
<details class="research-project__details"><summary>Details &amp; related work</summary><dl class="research-project__facts">
<div><dt>Participants</dt><dd>60 participants across children with autism spectrum disorder, typically developing children, and typically developing adults.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Methods</dt><dd>Functional transcranial Doppler ultrasound, language and face-processing tasks, and laterality index analysis.</dd></div>
<div><dt>My role</dt><dd>Designed E-Prime paradigms, collected recordings, and analyzed laterality indices for all participants as part of my master’s thesis.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Key finding</dt><dd>Language lateralization showed contrasting developmental patterns: a leftward tendency in typically developing children and a rightward tendency in children with autism. Face processing was consistently right-lateralized across groups.</dd></div>
</dl><p>E-Prime for task design and DopOSCCI for laterality analysis. Related work was presented at APSSLH in 2023.</p></details>
</article>

<article class="research-project" aria-labelledby="project-5-language-anxiety-among-exchange-students">
<p class="research-project__meta">Previous · Questionnaire-Based Assessment</p>
<h3 id="project-5-language-anxiety-among-exchange-students">Language Anxiety Among Exchange Students</h3>
<ul class="research-project__keywords" aria-label="Research keywords"><li>Second-Language Acquisition</li><li>Language Proficiency</li><li>Language Anxiety</li></ul>
<p class="research-project__summary">How does language anxiety relate to English proficiency among Mandarin-speaking exchange students?</p>
<details class="research-project__details"><summary>Details &amp; related work</summary><dl class="research-project__facts">
<div><dt>Participants</dt><dd>Mandarin-speaking exchange students during my UC Berkeley visiting period; independent research conducted Jan.–May 2025.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Methods</dt><dd>Questionnaire-based assessment and quantitative analysis.</dd></div>
<div><dt>My role</dt><dd>Independently designed and completed the study, from instrument development and recruitment to data collection, statistical analysis, and conference presentation.</dd></div>
<div><dt>Key finding</dt><dd>Language anxiety and proficiency did not show a simple inverse relationship. Associations varied across listening, speaking, reading, and writing, highlighting individual differences.</dd></div>
</dl><p>Advised by Prof. Joanna Reed. Presented at the International Conference on Educational Innovation in 2025.</p></details>
