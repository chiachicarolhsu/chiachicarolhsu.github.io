Hi! I’m Chia-Chi, and you can call me Carol.

I am a full-time research assistant at the Agile, Brain and Cognition Lab, National Tsing Hua University. My research focuses on **language and reading development in children**, particularly individual differences in **neural organization and brain–behavior relationships** across typical and atypical development. My training in special education motivated me to investigate why children differ in language and reading abilities and how these differences relate to the developing brain.

My research experience spans **fTCD, EEG/ERP, and fMRI**, providing methodological training across language, mathematical cognition, and memory research. Building on this work, I am interested in investigating **how learning and experience shape developing neural systems and whether these neural changes relate to language and reading outcomes**.

    <details class="publication-card__details">
      <summary>Abstract &amp; keywords</summary>
      <div class="publication-card__expanded">
        <h4>Abstract — summary</h4>
        <p>Comparing 19 high school cycling athletes with 19 matched controls, this study examined multiplication verification using behavioral measures and event-related potentials. Athletes showed lower mathematical proficiency, poorer task performance, and larger posterior P300 responses. After adjusting for baseline mathematical proficiency, the P300 group difference was no longer statistically significant. These findings highlight domain-specific proficiency when interpreting neural differences and suggest that athletic expertise does not necessarily transfer to arithmetic skills.</p>
